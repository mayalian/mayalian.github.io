About

Who I Am

When I was little, I drew things I didn't understand yet , machinery, cranes, systems that moved in ways I couldn't explain. Drawing was how I tried to figure them out.

Then I discovered that drawing had limits. A drawing shows you what something looks like. It can't show you how it behaves when a person is depending on it. That gap is what pulled me into engineering.

I taught myself Python because I wanted to build something that moved. I built a rhythm game because I wanted to understand why some systems feel right and others don't. I went to NASA because I wanted to build visualizations that could help people imagine and design future human life beyond Earth. I built a financial platform because I wanted to help a city council member see his own city clearly enough to make better decisions.

The question has been the same every time: how do people understand complex systems? I've been asking it through drawing, through code, through research, and through competitive speech. I've never entirely belonged to one category. I think that's the most honest thing I can say about myself.
