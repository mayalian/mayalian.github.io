Who I Am

I rarely decide what I want to learn next. Most of the time, one project quietly leads me to another.

Drawing came first. Then I wanted my drawings to move, so I learned animation. Animation eventually wasn't enough, so I started building games. One research project turned into an interactive dashboard. Every project eventually reached a point where what I already knew wasn't enough.

I've never been interested in learning tools for their own sake. What keeps me going is reaching the point where I don't know how to build something yet, then figuring it out anyway.

That's how this portfolio ended up including digital art, game development, robotics, data visualization, and space exploration. They look unrelated. They've never felt that way to me.

I don't know exactly where that path will lead. I only know I'll probably keep following it.

Questions I'm Asking

The projects in this portfolio are different, but they keep leading me back to the same kinds of questions.

  • How do people understand things they can't directly see?
  • Why do small design decisions change what people notice?
  • How do interactive systems help people explore ideas instead of simply receiving answers?
  • How can technology make complicated information easier to understand without oversimplifying it?

I don't expect to answer all of these questions anytime soon. That's part of why I enjoy them.
