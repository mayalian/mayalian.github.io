About

Who I Am

I grew up drawing things I didn't fully understand yet — machinery, anatomy, systems that moved in ways I wanted to figure out. Drawing was how I processed the world before I had the vocabulary for what I was doing.

That habit eventually showed me its own limits. A drawing can show the surface of a system. It can't show how the system behaves when a person uses it under pressure. That gap is what pulled me into engineering.

Now I work at the intersection: visual thinking applied to systems that people depend on. The rover interface at NASA. The financial platform a city council member uses to understand his city. The rhythm game where the engineering problem wasn't the code — it was making the experience feel right.

I'm a competitive speaker, a robotics programmer, an animator, and a researcher. I've never entirely belonged to one category. I think that's the most honest thing I can say about myself.
